title: "billing & credits"
description: "how Geko meters usage, what it costs, and how credits work."
---

Geko runs on **one credit balance per organization**, and **one API key** spends it on either product — text-to-speech bills by input characters, speech-to-text by seconds of audio. There is no second balance to top up and nothing to switch between.

## Pricing

| product | rate | in credits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **text-to-speech** | **$0.04 per 1,000 characters** | 1 credit per character |
| **speech-to-text** (files) | **$0.36 per hour of audio** | 2.5 credits per audio-second (9,000/hour) |
| **speech-to-text** (live streaming) | **$0.864 per hour of audio** | 6 credits per audio-second (21,600/hour) |

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Credit value** | 25,000 credits = **$1.00** |
| **Free on signup** | **$1.00** (25,000 credits) for every new organization |

As a rough feel: **$1 ≈ 25,000 characters ≈ ~30 minutes** of synthesized Kazakh (it runs ~14 characters per second of audio), or **~2.8 hours** of file transcription, or **~1.2 hours** of live streaming.

### Why streaming costs more

Live streaming is billed at 2.4× the file rate because it costs that much more to serve, not as a surcharge for the feature. Keeping a partial transcript current means re-decoding the audio that hasn't been committed yet on every update, so a second of speech passes through the model roughly six times before it is finalised.

**If you have a file, use [`transcribe()`](/stt) rather than streaming.** It is more accurate on long audio — the model sees whole utterances instead of a growing tail — and it costs a third as much. Streaming exists for the case where you cannot wait for the speaker to finish: a voice agent that has to reply, or live captions.

Credits are denominated in characters, which is why a transcription's `credits_charged` is a character-equivalent rather than a count of anything in the audio.

Only **successful** requests are billed — synthesis on the **input** character count (`X-Tokay-Chars`), transcription on audio duration rounded up (`X-Seta-Audio-Seconds` and `X-Seta-Credits`). The catalog endpoints — `/v1/models`, `/v1/voices`, `/health` — are open and **free** on both hosts.

## Running out

When your balance hits zero, **both** products return **HTTP 429** with a `detail` telling you you're out of credits — it is one balance, so synthesis and transcription stop together. Top up in the [console](https://app.geko.sh). The SDK surfaces this as a `GekoError` with `status === 429` and does **not** retry it (see [Errors & retries](/sdk/errors)) — a retry can't create credits.

## Managing credits

- **Balance & history:** view your current balance and per-request usage in the [console](https://app.geko.sh).
- **Top up:** add credits in the console's billing section.
- **Estimate before you send:** both directions can be priced without a request.

```ts
// text-to-speech — 1 credit per input character
const ttsCost = (text: string) => text.length / 25_000; // USD

// speech-to-text, files — matches the server's rounding, ceil(seconds × 2.5)
geko.stt.estimateCredits(3600); // 9000 credits === $0.36 for an audio-hour

// speech-to-text, live streaming — ceil(seconds × 6)
geko.stt.estimateStreamCredits(3600); // 21600 credits === $0.864 for an audio-hour
```

<Note>
  `tts.stream` and both OpenAI-compatible endpoints bill identically to their native counterparts — delivery and request shape change, price doesn't. **The one exception is live speech-to-text** (`stt.stream`), which is a different rate because it is genuinely more expensive to serve. Pricing is subject to change; the [console](https://app.geko.sh) always shows the current rate.
</Note>
